This report analyzes annual territorial carbon dioxide (CO₂) emissions using data published by Our World in Data, with the Global Carbon Budget (2025) listed as the underlying source. It transforms the dataset into three complementary visualizations: a ranked horizontal bar chart comparing selected countries in 2024, a share chart comparing those selected countries with the remainder of the global total, and a line chart showing rounded exploratory trends for China, the United States and India across selected years.

The visualizations are designed to answer distinct questions. The horizontal bar chart makes category comparisons and rankings easy to read. The share chart communicates the approximate scale of selected emitters relative to the global total. The line chart highlights that major emitters have followed different broad trajectories over time. Together, the charts support a concise narrative: emissions are unevenly distributed, China is the largest emitter among the selected countries in the 2024 comparison, and the selected countries do not share identical trends.

The project uses Python, pandas and Matplotlib. The repository contains the report, CSV data files, chart images, source code and a README with setup and reproduction instructions. Each chart includes a title, units, labels and a short explanation of its purpose. The report documents the data source, preparation steps, interpretation, limitations and future improvements.

Important limitation: the 2024 country values are rounded, and the historical trend values are rounded exploratory points for communication rather than precision estimates. The source indicator measures territorial emissions from fossil fuels and industry and excludes land-use change; it does not represent consumption-based emissions or per-capita emissions. Future work will use the full year-by-year source CSV and add per-capita comparisons and an interactive dashboard. Source: https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/annual-co2-emissions-per-country
